\textbf{Question 1.} \textit{(§2.2 Modelling Manual Control) Nyquist diagram (from Bode diagram) for controller in series with plant.}

\vspace{1em}

\textbf{System Model:} The simplified aircraft dynamics satisfy
$$ \ddot{y}(t) + M\dot{y}(t) = Nx(t) $$
Taking the Laplace transform (with zero initial conditions), the plant transfer function is:
$$ G(s) = \frac{N}{s^2 + Ms} = \frac{N}{s(s+M)} $$
This represents an integrator ($1/s$) in series with a first-order lag ($1/(s+M)$). The human controller is modelled as a proportional gain $k$ with pure time delay $D$:
$$ K(s) = ke^{-sD} $$

\textbf{Open-Loop Transfer Function:}
$$ L(s) = K(s)G(s) = \frac{kN \cdot e^{-sD}}{s(s + M)} $$

\textbf{Frequency Response Analysis:} Substituting $s = j\omega$:
$$ L(j\omega) = \frac{kN \cdot e^{-j\omega D}}{j\omega(j\omega + M)} $$

The denominator can be written as:
$$ j\omega(j\omega + M) = j\omega \cdot |j\omega + M| \cdot e^{j\arctan(\omega/M)} = \omega\sqrt{\omega^2+M^2} \cdot e^{j(\pi/2 + \arctan(\omega/M))} $$

Therefore, the magnitude and phase are:
$$ |L(j\omega)| = \frac{kN}{\omega\sqrt{\omega^2+M^2}}, \quad \angle L(j\omega) = -\frac{\pi}{2} - \arctan\left(\frac{\omega}{M}\right) - \omega D $$

\textbf{Asymptotic Behaviour:}
\begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Low frequency} ($\omega \to 0$): $|L| \to \infty$, $\angle L \to -\pi/2$
        \item \textbf{High frequency} ($\omega \to \infty$): $|L| \to 0$, $\angle L \to -\infty$
\end{itemize}

Substituting experimental values ($M=N=10$, $k=0.8$, $D=1$s):
$$ L(j\omega) = \frac{8 e^{-j\omega}}{j\omega(j\omega + 10)} $$

From the Bode diagram (Figure~\ref{fig:bode_manual}), the gain crossover frequency $\omega_{gc} \approx 0.8$ rad/s. At this frequency:
$$ \angle L(j \cdot 0.8) = -90^\circ - \arctan(0.08) - 0.8 \times \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} \approx -90^\circ - 4.6^\circ - 45.8^\circ \approx -140^\circ $$
The phase margin is $\text{PM} = 180^\circ - 140^\circ = 40^\circ$ (approximately $45^\circ$ from graph).

\textbf{Question 2.} \textit{(§2.2 Modelling Manual Control) Are you using any integral action? Give a brief explanation. What does this imply about the accuracy of the model of the human controller?}

\vspace{1em}

\textbf{Yes.} In Figure~\ref{fig:step_manual}, after the step disturbance, the error signal $e(t) \to 0$ while the control input $u(t)$ settles to a non-zero constant. For a purely proportional controller $u = ke$, this would be impossible---zero error implies zero output. The sustained $u \neq 0$ when $e = 0$ indicates integral action:
$$ u(t) = k_p e(t) + k_i \int_0^t e(\tau) \, d\tau $$
The integral term accumulates past error, allowing $u_{ss} \neq 0$ even when $e_{ss} = 0$.

\textbf{Model accuracy:} The simple model $K(s) = ke^{-sD}$ is purely proportional and neglects this low-frequency integral behaviour. It captures fast reactions but not the pilot's adaptive error accumulation.

\textbf{Question 3.} \textit{(§2.3 Pilot Induced Oscillation) Explain the oscillation of the feedback loop. How does your observed period of oscillation compare to the theoretical prediction?}

\vspace{1em}

PIO occurs when the pilot controller's time delay introduces sufficient phase lag that the total loop phase reaches $-180^\circ$ at a frequency where the loop gain exceeds unity. The PIO aircraft has $G_1(s) = c/(Ts+1)^3$ which contributes up to $-270^\circ$ phase lag, plus the pilot delay $e^{-sD}$ adds $-\omega D$ radians. At the phase crossover frequency $\omega_{pc}$ where $\angle L(j\omega_{pc}) = -180^\circ$, if $|L(j\omega_{pc})| > 1$, the system is unstable and oscillates at frequency $\omega_{pc}$. From Bode (Figure~\ref{fig:pio_bode}), $\omega_{pc} \approx 0.8$ rad/s, giving theoretical period $T = 2\pi/\omega_{pc} \approx 7.85$s. The observed period ($\approx 5$s) is shorter, suggesting the pilot controller adapts their gain/delay under oscillatory conditions.

\textbf{Question 4.} \textit{(§2.3 Pilot Induced Oscillation) Can you give a rough guideline to the control designer to make PIO less likely?}

\vspace{1em}

To prevent PIO, ensure adequate phase margin at frequencies where the pilot is active ($< 1$ Hz):
\begin{itemize}
        \item Avoid plants with high phase lag at low frequencies
        \item Design for phase margin $> 45^\circ$ at the expected pilot crossover frequency
        \item Consider rate limiters to prevent high-frequency pilot inputs
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Question 5.} \textit{(§2.4 Sinusoidal Disturbances) Was your manual input able to reduce the error (as compared to providing no input)?}

\vspace{1em}

\textbf{No.} The closed-loop response to disturbance $d$ is $y = \dfrac{G}{1+KG} d$. For disturbance rejection, we need $|1+KG| > 1$.

At 0.66 Hz ($\omega = 4.15$ rad/s), pilot delay adds phase $-\omega D = -4.15 \times 1 \approx -238^\circ$. Combined with plant phase ($\approx -90^\circ$ from Bode), total phase approaches $-330^\circ$, meaning $KG$ points nearly opposite to $-1$. This makes $|1+KG| < 1$, so the disturbance is actually \emph{amplified} rather than attenuated. Manual control worsens the oscillation.

\textbf{Question 6.} \textit{(§2.5 An Unstable Aircraft) Nyquist diagram for $G_2(s)$.}

\vspace{1em}

The unstable aircraft has transfer function:
$$ G_2(s) = \frac{2}{sT-1} $$
with one unstable (RHP) pole at $s = 1/T > 0$.

\textbf{Frequency Response Derivation:} Substituting $s = j\omega$:
$$ G_2(j\omega) = \frac{2}{j\omega T - 1} $$

To find real and imaginary parts, multiply by conjugate:
$$ G_2(j\omega) = \frac{2}{-(1 - j\omega T)} = \frac{-2(1 + j\omega T)}{(1 - j\omega T)(1 + j\omega T)} = \frac{-2(1 + j\omega T)}{1 + \omega^2 T^2} $$

Therefore:
$$ \text{Re}[G_2(j\omega)] = \frac{-2}{1 + \omega^2 T^2}, \quad \text{Im}[G_2(j\omega)] = \frac{-2\omega T}{1 + \omega^2 T^2} $$

\textbf{Geometric Interpretation:} Letting $x = \text{Re}$ and $y = \text{Im}$:
$$ (x+1)^2 + y^2 = \left(\frac{-2}{1+\omega^2T^2} + 1\right)^2 + \left(\frac{-2\omega T}{1+\omega^2T^2}\right)^2 = 1 $$
This is a circle centered at $(-1, 0)$ with radius 1.

\textbf{Boundary Points:}
\begin{itemize}
        \item At $\omega = 0$: $G_2(0) = -2$ (leftmost point on real axis)
        \item As $\omega \to \infty$: $G_2 \to 0$ (approaches origin)
\end{itemize}

The locus is traced clockwise as $\omega$ increases from $0$ to $\infty$.

\textbf{Question 7.} \textit{(§2.5 An Unstable Aircraft) Explain, using the Nyquist criterion, why the feedback system is stable with a proportional gain greater than 0.5.}

\vspace{1em}

\textbf{Nyquist Criterion:} For a system with $P$ open-loop poles in the RHP, closed-loop stability requires $N = -P$ encirclements of $-1/K$, where $N > 0$ is counter-clockwise.

Here $P = 1$ (one unstable pole), so we need $N = -1$: one \emph{clockwise} encirclement of $-1/K$.

The Nyquist locus is a circle from $-2$ to $0$ on the real axis, traversed clockwise. For $-1/K$ to be encircled clockwise:
$$ -2 < -\frac{1}{K} < 0 \implies 0 < \frac{1}{K} < 2 \implies K > 0.5 $$

For $K > 0.5$, the point $-1/K$ lies inside the circle, giving the required clockwise encirclement for stability.

\textbf{Question 8.} \textit{(§2.5 An Unstable Aircraft) Sketch of a Nyquist diagram for $G_2(s)$ with a small time delay $D$.}

\vspace{1em}

With delay, the transfer function becomes $G_2(s)e^{-sD}$. The frequency response is:
$$ G_2(j\omega)e^{-j\omega D} = \frac{2}{j\omega T - 1} \cdot e^{-j\omega D} $$

The delay contributes phase $-\omega D$ without changing magnitude:
$$ |G_2(j\omega)e^{-j\omega D}| = |G_2(j\omega)|, \quad \angle(G_2 e^{-j\omega D}) = \angle G_2 - \omega D $$

\textbf{Effect on Nyquist Locus:}
\begin{itemize}
        \item At $\omega = 0$: No change; still starts at $G_2(0) = -2$.
        \item As $\omega$ increases: Additional clockwise rotation by $\omega D$ radians.
        \item The circular locus becomes a spiral curving inward toward the origin.
\end{itemize}

For large $D$, the spiral may cross $-1/K$ multiple times, potentially destabilizing the system. This explains why manual stabilization becomes impossible when pilot delay $D$ exceeds the system time constant $T$.

\textbf{Question 9.} \textit{(§3.4 Integrator Wind-up) Explain how you calculated the bound on $Q$.}

\vspace{1em}

\textbf{PID Controller:} $u(t) = K_p \left( e + \dfrac{1}{T_i}\displaystyle\int_0^t e \, d\tau + T_d \dfrac{de}{dt} \right)$

\textbf{Steady-state analysis:} After settling, $e_{ss} = 0$ and $\dot{e} = 0$. To cancel a step disturbance $d$, we need $u_{ss} = d$. Only the integral term contributes:
$$ u_{ss} = \frac{K_p}{T_i} I_{ss} = d \implies I_{ss} = \frac{d \cdot T_i}{K_p} $$

\textbf{Minimum bound:} For $d = 2$, $T_i = 1.0$, $K_p = 7.2$:
$$ Q = \frac{2 \times 1.0}{7.2} = \frac{5}{18} \approx 0.278 $$

Setting $Q$ smaller would prevent full disturbance rejection (steady-state error). Setting $Q$ larger allows more wind-up overshoot.
